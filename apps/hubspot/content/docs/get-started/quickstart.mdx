This starter brings together three different kinds of source: a sponsor-research application, a HubSpot skills library and an orchestration setup guide. They are examples to learn from, not evidence that your portal is connected or that a sponsor has agreed to participate.

The examples below were checked against repository content at fixed commits. They were not executed against HubSpot or a research provider. Consult your installed CLI's help before relying on a command, and use an authorised test portal for the first write.

## Choose a bounded outcome

Begin with one company, one research question and one deliverable. A useful first task is: “Read the supplied evidence for this company, identify unknowns and prepare an account brief. Do not send a message or modify CRM records.” This defines a result without silently authorising outreach.

The sponsor example uses Nocturnal Valley as a client-supplied campaign. Its attendance is disputed, its available inventory is not supplied and its sponsor-conflict list is incomplete. Treat those as first-class states, not missing copy to fill. The campaign dates are in September 2026, so the example is historical by October 2026; it is not a current sales opportunity.

## Inspect the HubSpot surface

These source commands discover supported object types and the contact schema:

```bash
hubspot objects types
hubspot properties list --type contacts
```

The executable is `hubspot`, not the separate `hs` developer CLI. The skills repository does not ship the executable, authentication implementation or a complete installation guide for it. Have an administrator provision the appropriate CLI and account access; do not substitute a similarly named package or guess a login command.

## Read a known company

The source uses this exact example. Replace the illustrative domain with an owner-approved canonical domain before running it:

```bash
hubspot objects search --type companies --filter "domain=acme.com" \
  --properties name,domain,industry,annualrevenue,hubspot_owner_id
```

An empty result means the lookup found no matching record; it does not prove the company does not exist. Multiple results require identity review before any association or update. Search examples return a page, not a guarantee of complete coverage.

## Produce a reviewable result

Keep the brief small: resolved company identity, cited activity, existing CRM context, unknowns and next action. A research result must retain its source and observation date. A CRM match must retain its record ID. Neither a company's category nor a retrieved employee title proves that the company has an open sponsorship budget.

Stop at the requested boundary. A brief is not a campaign, a draft is not a sent email and a dry run is not a write. Continue with the [CRM brief recipe](/docs/recipes/crm-brief), the [sponsor qualification recipe](/docs/recipes/sponsor-qualification) or the [safe-write guide](/docs/guides/safe-writes).
