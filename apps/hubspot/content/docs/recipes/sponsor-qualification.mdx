---
title: Qualify a sponsor without overstating fit
description: Turn one target row into a cited, bounded decision with visible unknowns.
---

Use this recipe when you need a sponsor shortlist rather than a speculative list of brands. The output is one reviewable dossier per company, not a promise of interest or a sent pitch.

The source example is historical. Recheck dates and evidence before applying its approach to a current campaign.

## 1. Resolve identity before research

Select one source row with a supplied exact domain. BeatBox Beverages is a useful fixture because its row includes a domain and an Electric Forest lead. That lead is a starting citation from the repository, not a fresh verification performed by this documentation.

If the row names a parent or distributor ambiguously, stop for the owner's choice. If it lacks a domain, keep it as a lead. Do not resolve a convenient similarly named business and silently substitute it.

[Source: target rows](https://github.com/Git-Godssoldier/opulent-sponsor-context-showcase/blob/662ddba58c42f28ae71d0aca1a9cbb7794ec3d26/campaigns/nocturnal-valley/targets.csv#L2-L14).

## 2. Gather one dated activation

Read the cited public page and record the exact company, activity, event, date, quotation and URL. Distinguish page publication date from activation date. An undated logo wall may support background context, but it cannot by itself show a live budget inside a defined recency window.

The sponsor project offers these source commands for a local checkout:

```bash
npm run discover -- --mass --similarity exact
```

This selects the documented exact-similarity option; it is not a byte-for-byte quotation of the source's optional-argument notation. Mass discovery expands beyond one company, so omit it when your authority covers only a supplied target. The signal step is separate: use the real public page you inspected and the source's documented URL argument. Discovery may call a paid research provider when credentials are present. Inspect the source, account scope and cost authority before running it; no provider calls were made for this starter.

## 3. Explain fit and the case against it

A category match alone is weak evidence. A stronger case combines a dated activation with retrieved regional presence or audience overlap. State counter-evidence: category exclusivity, an age-gated activation format, an uncertain market fit or a missing inventory answer may change the recommendation.

Company activity does not prove an individual employee commissioned it. General search can nominate a profile; only exact-profile retrieval can resolve the person in the source model. A departed employee's current employer needs its own activity evidence.

## 4. Apply gates before drafting

Keep compliance blocks and owner-decision holds separate. The fixture allows research on blocked companies but refuses a draft. Keep the exclusion check unverified while the owner's exclusion list is missing. Do not convert supplied audience estimates into verified facts, and omit disputed attendance entirely.

A useful dossier ends with: fit band, evidence, counter-evidence, unknowns, gate state and named next action. “Needs the owner's category rule” is a valid result.

## Completion check

The source validator checks evidence requirements for strong and plausible bands, rejects clearance without the exclusion gate, and permits only draft-only send state. Read those checks as the example application's boundaries, not as legal or commercial clearance for your campaign.

Before creating a CRM record, follow the [seed mapping](/docs/guides/seed-data) and [safe-write flow](/docs/guides/safe-writes). Before publishing a dossier, remove contact values and any client material you lack permission to redistribute.
