---
title: 'Move profile-based contact evidence into HubSpot safely'
description: 'Separate person matching, employment context, email verification and phone evidence before proposing updates to HubSpot contact records.'
---

A professional profile is an identity clue, not proof that an email address is current or a phone number belongs to the person. The workflow should answer four different questions: which person is this, which employment context matters, which contact channels have supporting evidence and which fields may be written into HubSpot. Combining those decisions into a single verified flag hides the most important errors.

Source: [Deepline verified contact details from profiles](https://deepline.com/docs/guides/verified-contact-details-from-profiles). Read date: **2026-10-18**. This is an original offline implementation plan; **no live HubSpot execution** or contact-data lookup has been performed. Source licensing is not established, so source prose and code are omitted.

## Deepline-specific context vs HubSpot adaptation

The source distinguishes provider search from enrichment, current-employer context from historical events, and channel verification from permission to contact. Deepline's catalog references do not guarantee that a particular provider returns phone data or that the organisation is entitled to use it.

The proposed HubSpot adaptation retains a contact as the person record, preserves company and deal relationships separately, and stores independent evidence states for email and phone. This is a recommended architecture, not a claim that HubSpot verifies provider data automatically. Do not use real contact details in public examples or retained test fixtures.

## Prerequisites and licensing

Confirm the legitimate business purpose, provider access rights, geographic restrictions and retention policy before obtaining personal data. Use only authorised profile sources. Have an administrator inspect application scopes and record access for contacts, companies and any required associations. [HubSpot's scope documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/apps/developer-platform/build-apps/authentication/scopes) describes endpoint-specific permissions; a successful connection is not evidence of permission for every intended action.

The baseline design uses standard objects and an external evidence register. Custom objects require Enterprise, and association labels require Professional or Enterprise under [HubSpot's object guidance](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-objects). If unavailable, keep relationship roles in the external evidence record and use only supported standard associations. Provider licences and verification charges are separate from HubSpot subscriptions.

## Contact model and evidence states

Inspect the current definitions of `email`, `firstname`, `lastname`, `jobtitle`, `phone` and `mobilephone` before deciding which fields are writable. [The contacts API guide](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/objects/contacts/guide) documents creating, retrieving and updating contact properties. A populated phone property is storage, not evidence of number ownership.

Proposed custom contact properties, not built-in fields, are `profile_identity_state`, `employment_evidence_date`, `work_email_evidence_state` and `phone_evidence_state`. Store raw provider statuses and source observations externally so a compact CRM status cannot erase uncertainty. Define separate enumerations for person matched, employment unresolved, email accepted and phone ownership unknown. Do not reuse one generic verified value across these dimensions. Create metadata only after checking [property types and definitions](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/properties/guide).

## Practical implementation steps

1. **Preserve source identity.** Keep the original profile URL, source-system person ID and observation time. Remove tracking parameters only when doing so preserves the profile identifier. Reject company pages presented as person profiles.
2. **Resolve the person.** Compare name, role, employer and other permitted context. Common names require corroboration. Search the existing HubSpot contact set, then retain a reviewed mapping to the chosen record ID. Do not create a new contact merely because a search result has not appeared immediately; [CRM search documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/search-the-crm) warns of indexing delay.
3. **Choose the employment frame.** Decide whether the task concerns the person's present role or an earlier event. A job change can invalidate an old work-email candidate without invalidating the identity match. Preserve historical deal participation instead of rewriting history to match the new employer.
4. **Evaluate email independently.** Match the candidate address to the intended employer and retain verifier status, timestamp and catch-all or unknown results. A mailbox check at the former employer does not establish current-role suitability. Keep the existing HubSpot email unchanged until the proposed replacement passes identity and employment review.
5. **Evaluate phone independently.** Retain country context, provider and observation time. Formatting is not proof of reachability, ownership or mobile status. Leave those attributes unknown when evidence is absent, even if email passed.
6. **Prepare an explicit change set.** Show old and proposed contact values and the evidence for each. Read company associations before adding a new relationship. Use the [associations API](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/associations/associate-records/guide) according to the configured object direction and labels; do not indiscriminately remove historical relationships.

## Approval boundary

Research and deterministic normalisation may be pre-authorised. Replacing a primary email, changing employer associations, merging identities or releasing a phone number for calling requires the organisation's defined reviewer and usage policy. Contactability is not consent. This workflow must not enrol anyone into sequences, dial a number or send a message simply because data was accepted.

## Failure handling

Hold conflicting employers and ambiguous people with a clear reason and repair owner. Distinguish no-result from provider errors. Preserve completed lookup receipts so retries do not repeatedly purchase the same evidence. For an uncertain HubSpot write, retrieve the known contact ID and reconcile the individual field before retrying. Never resolve a duplicate identity by discarding whichever record is less convenient.

## Validation and release criteria

Use fictional examples covering an exact match, a common name, a recent job change, a verified old-employer mailbox and an unowned phone number. Assert that accepted email cannot promote phone to verified and that unresolved identity blocks both channels. Report email coverage, phone coverage and overlap against the original profile population; do not add channel counts without removing overlap. A future controlled pilot must verify every changed property and association by read-back and show that reruns create no duplicate contacts. These are planned checks, not live results.
