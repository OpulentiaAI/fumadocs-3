---
title: 'Design a pre-send email verification gate for HubSpot contacts'
description: 'Keep mailbox evidence, person identity and communication permission separate when preparing an approved outbound cohort.'
---

Email verification answers a technical question at a point in time; it does not answer whether a contact should receive a campaign. Build three separate gates for person identity, mailbox evidence and communication eligibility. Only a record that passes the approved policy for all three belongs in a candidate sending cohort, and even that cohort needs a final eligibility check at the sending boundary.

Source: [Deepline automated email verification guide](https://deepline.com/docs/guides/automated-email-verification). Source read date: **2026-10-18**. This is an original offline implementation plan with **no live HubSpot execution**, verification requests or email sending. The source's damaged cost-example formatting was checked against its raw HTML; no ambiguous figures are reused.

## Deepline-specific guidance versus this HubSpot adaptation

Deepline's guide describes inspecting a verifier's contract and orchestrating a checked gate. Its tool discovery and provider execution belong to Deepline. This HubSpot adaptation proposes contact-level operational states and a separate decision receipt. It does not claim that HubSpot performs third-party mailbox verification or treats a custom verified flag as campaign permission.

The distinction is material: HubSpot's [marketing email consent policy](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/understand-opt-in-consent-for-email) requires verifiable permission and says addresses from prospecting or enrichment sources alone do not qualify. A favourable technical mailbox result cannot override that requirement.

## Prerequisites and licensing

Select an authorised contact sample, an approved verification provider, its current response definitions and a documented suppression source. Have the privacy owner define eligibility for the intended communication type and jurisdiction. Marketing email, one-to-one sales activity and other channels are not interchangeable; this plan does not make a legal determination for any of them.

Inspect [contact properties and identifiers](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/objects/contacts/guide) and the proposed custom property types before implementation. The [workflows tool](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows) requires a qualifying subscription and appropriate action permissions if used. HubSpot's [double opt-in feature](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/marketing-email/set-up-double-opt-in-for-emails) has separate availability rules and concerns email confirmation, not third-party mailbox scoring. Do not buy a higher tier merely on the assumption that it replaces a verification provider.

## Implementation steps

1. **Preserve the actual address.** Key the row by contact object type, record ID and the exact address revision being evaluated. Keep the raw address and a comparison representation. Do not strip dots or plus suffixes globally: transformations can change identity outside providers that explicitly document equivalence. Check duplicate inputs before incurring lookup cost.
2. **Resolve identity and suppression first.** Verify that the address belongs to the intended person and current employer where that matters. Read the relevant communication permissions from the authoritative source. HubSpot documents that [subscription preferences attach to the email address](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/manage-your-subscription-preferences-and-types), so replacing the contact ID is not a legitimate way to escape an opt-out.
3. **Map provider responses deliberately.** Proposed properties include `ops_mailbox_state`, `ops_mailbox_checked_at`, `ops_identity_state`, `ops_send_gate_state` and `ops_verification_receipt_url`. Suggested decisions are accepted, held, rejected and operational retry. These are local categories, not a universal provider schema. Keep catch-all and unknown outcomes visible; a network timeout provides no mailbox conclusion.
4. **Bind the decision to the address.** Store the verifier's original status, source time, acceptance-policy version and eligible communication type in the receipt. Use the associated company ID only for employer context. Do not add new company associations or change `email` as a side effect. If the address changes, invalidate the old decision rather than carrying its verified state forward.
5. **Prepare a separate send handoff.** A later approved process may publish only gate metadata to HubSpot after rereading the contact. It must recheck suppression and freshness immediately before any campaign action. Never map accepted verification to opted-in status, marketing-contact status, sequence enrolment or automatic sending. Those are separate decisions and permissions.

## Approval boundary and failure handling

This guide authorises neither verification spend nor CRM writes nor sending. A pilot needs explicit approval of its sample, provider budget, policy and intended channel. Changes to subscription preferences require their own valid permission evidence; no reviewer may substitute a provider score for the contact's action.

Use bounded retries for temporary provider errors and retain the original failure. Hold conflicting verifier observations instead of shopping for a favourable answer. When a suppression source is unavailable, fail closed for candidate sending rather than assuming eligibility. If a contact unsubscribes after the batch was built, the final gate must remove it even when all other evidence remains current.

## Validation and acceptance

Prepare fixtures for an eligible verified address, catch-all, invalid address, timeout, duplicate, changed employer, later opt-out and changed primary email. Confirm that only the first remains eligible under the example conservative policy. Test that rerunning the same address revision creates no duplicate candidate export and that held rows cannot leak into the accepted file. Count usable eligible identities, holds by reason and verification-to-use delay; measure actual delivery separately. These are offline acceptance criteria, not live HubSpot results or an inbox-placement guarantee.
