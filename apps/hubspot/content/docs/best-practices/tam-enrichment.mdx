title: 'Build a governed target-account universe in HubSpot'
description: 'Turn a market definition into evidence-backed HubSpot company records without confusing missing data, poor fit and duplicate accounts.'
---

An addressable market is a defined population, not a spreadsheet of every company a provider can return. Before importing accounts into HubSpot, decide whether the unit is a legal entity, a trading brand, a local office or a corporate group. That decision changes how domains, subsidiaries and territory ownership should be interpreted. Enrichment should make the population more usable without quietly changing its boundaries.

Source: [Deepline TAM enrichment](https://deepline.com/docs/guides/tam-enrichment). Read date: **2026-10-18**. This is an original offline implementation plan; **no live HubSpot execution** or CRM write has been performed. Source licensing is not established, so no source prose or code is reproduced.

## Deepline-specific context vs HubSpot adaptation

The source treats identity resolution, accepted fit fields, coverage and provider costs as separate decisions. Its Deepline-related provider and CLI references describe a possible enrichment route; they do not establish an installed HubSpot integration or a universal match rate.

The HubSpot adaptation below uses company records as the operational destination, with an external evidence table for candidates and rejected matches. The proposed segmentation states and properties are design recommendations, not built-in HubSpot TAM features. A company being present in the CRM does not imply that it is qualified or eligible for outreach.

## Prerequisites and licensing

Obtain approval for the market definition, permitted provider sources and intended uses of the resulting data. Export a small existing-company reference set with HubSpot record IDs, domains and account owners. Verify the installed application's read and write scopes against the relevant endpoints; [HubSpot's scopes documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/apps/developer-platform/build-apps/authentication/scopes) explains that scopes grant access to specific endpoints and data.

The baseline design does not need custom objects. Custom objects require an Enterprise subscription, while association labels require Professional or Enterprise according to [HubSpot's object guidance](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-objects). Standard workflows and their available actions depend on the portal's subscriptions; [workflow creation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows) lists the supported Professional and Enterprise products. Keep orchestration external if the required actions are unavailable. Do not assume that API access includes enrichment data or provider licences.

## Company model and evidence contract

Use the documented company properties `name` and `domain`, plus the portal's current owner field and relevant firmographics after inspecting their definitions. [The companies API guide](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/objects/companies/guide) recommends including a domain, but this is not a guarantee that every creation path prevents duplicates. Maintain an explicit source-to-HubSpot-ID mapping.

Proposed custom company properties, not built-in fields, are `tam_cohort_id` for the approved population version, `tam_fit_state` with accepted, rejected and unknown options, `tam_evidence_date` for the assessment date, and `tam_review_reason` for unresolved cases. Create them only after checking property limits, internal names and types using the [properties API documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/properties/guide). Keep detailed field-by-field sources outside the CRM rather than overwriting one URL every time a new field arrives.

## Practical implementation steps

1. **Freeze a cohort specification.** Record geography, business model, size thresholds, exclusions and the effective date. State whether parent and subsidiary companies count separately. Keep the original candidate population so later enrichment cannot silently remove difficult rows.
2. **Resolve identity before fit.** Normalise domains conservatively, then compare legal name, website and location. Treat a shared group domain as a review case when the target unit is a subsidiary. Never merge two companies merely because their names are similar.
3. **Build accepted field observations.** For each company and fit field, retain the provider, retrieval time, raw value, accepted value and reason. Convert employee bands only with an explicit mapping. An absent employee count remains unknown rather than zero.
4. **Apply a versioned fit policy.** Evaluate accepted observations against the cohort specification. Keep identity confidence separate from fit. A well-matched company with insufficient size evidence is unknown, not rejected.
5. **Prepare a HubSpot proposal.** Resolve existing company IDs before suggesting new records. Include the old and proposed values, the evidence date and the cohort version. Associate contacts or deals only when that relationship has independent evidence; a matching email domain is not proof of a particular buying opportunity.
6. **Release an approved subset.** After authorisation in a future deployment, update only the agreed field allowlist and read each affected company back by ID. Store record IDs and the observed result in the external cohort receipt. Preserve sales-owned fields and existing historical associations.

## Approval boundary

RevOps may pre-authorise deterministic normalisation and updates to designated enrichment fields. New company creation, record merges, territory reassignment and changes to lifecycle or opportunity data require an explicit business policy and an accountable approver. Marketing activation is a separate decision: belonging to the target market does not establish consent, lawful basis or a permitted sending channel.

## Failure handling

Quarantine conflicting domains and legal identities with a named research owner. Distinguish a provider outage from a genuine no-match result so market coverage does not appear to shrink during an incident. Cache completed provider receipts and cap fallback spending. If a HubSpot update times out, reconcile the company by its known ID before retrying; do not create a replacement company to make the run look complete.

## Validation and release criteria

Use fictional fixtures for a single-domain company, two subsidiaries sharing a domain, an existing customer excluded from acquisition, a stale size observation and an unresolved account. Confirm that every candidate receives one disposition and that input counts reconcile to accepted, rejected and unknown populations. Check a stratified sample across geography and company size, not just the easiest matches. Report usable-account coverage against the original population, cost per accepted account and unresolved cases by age. A future release passes only after a duplicate rerun produces no extra companies and read-back confirms the intended fields and associations. These checks are a test plan, not results from a live portal.
