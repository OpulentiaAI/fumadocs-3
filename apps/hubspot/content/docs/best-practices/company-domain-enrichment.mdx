---
title: 'Resolve a company domain before enriching HubSpot'
description: 'Turn a website into a defensible company match while preserving subsidiary ambiguity, profile evidence and employee-count definitions.'
---

A website is an input to account resolution, not a complete account identity. The same domain may represent a group, a regional operation or a product brand, while one operating company may own several domains. Resolve the business that your HubSpot record represents before using a provider response to populate company properties or choose a sales segment.

Source: [Deepline company-domain enrichment guide](https://deepline.com/docs/guides/company-domain-enrichment). Source read date: **2026-10-18**. This original offline implementation plan includes **no live HubSpot execution**. Source code and source prose are not reproduced.

## Deepline-specific guidance versus this HubSpot adaptation

The source distinguishes company identity from field acceptance, checks company-profile links against website evidence and preserves the meaning of employee counts. Its named enrichment providers and checked execution path are Deepline-specific. This adaptation recommends how to carry those decisions into HubSpot company records without assuming that HubSpot performs legal-entity matching.

The documented platform foundation is the [Companies API](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/objects/companies/guide), which describes company properties, record retrieval, updates and associations. HubSpot recommends including domain for duplicate avoidance. That recommendation does not prove that a domain uniquely identifies every subsidiary in your commercial account model.

## Prerequisites and licensing

Start with an approved website list, existing company IDs where available, a definition of the target entity and provider rights covering the intended use. Obtain a maintained domain parser and preserve the submitted URL. Decide whether the sales account is the operating company, parent or region before evaluating any candidates.

Inspect company property definitions and allowed option values using the [Properties API documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/properties/guide). The plan uses ordinary company records and proposed custom properties; it does not require a custom object. If native workflows will later trigger resolution or routing, confirm a qualifying Professional or Enterprise subscription and action-specific requirements in the [workflow documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows). No portal entitlement has been verified here.

## Implementation steps

1. **Preserve and parse the input.** Retain the raw URL, extract the hostname and remove paths and tracking parameters from the comparison key. Keep meaningful subdomains until the identity policy permits collapsing them. Do not derive a registrable domain by taking the final two labels. Consumer email domains do not establish an employer.
2. **Retrieve existing candidates.** Prefer a supplied HubSpot company ID. Otherwise search for domain and name evidence, then inspect every plausible candidate before proposing creation. HubSpot's [CRM Search documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/search-the-crm) describes property filtering and warns that newly changed records may take time to appear. A transient empty search is not proof that no record exists.
3. **Verify the entity and profile.** Compare the candidate website, trading name, geographic scope and provider record ID. Inspect a professional-network company profile's actual website context; do not invent a profile URL from a name. Record redirects separately because acquisitions and rebrands may connect different entities over time.
4. **Keep field definitions intact.** Suggested custom properties `ops_domain_match_state`, `ops_company_profile_url`, `ops_employee_range` and `ops_employee_measure` retain the decision and supporting meaning; they are proposed names, not HubSpot defaults. Store an observed range as a range. Do not average a parent's headcount with a subsidiary estimate or use a midpoint as an exact employee count.
5. **Prepare a minimal proposal.** Pair the resolved company ID with accepted fields, rejected candidates, evidence dates and the original CRM snapshot. Preserve contact and deal associations. A valid profile link can be accepted while employee-based segmentation remains on hold. Any later approved update should be reread and reconciled by ID, not judged from a search result alone.

## Approval boundary and failure handling

A reviewer resolves multiple plausible accounts, company creation, parent/subsidiary interpretation and domain replacements. Existing owner, lifecycle stage, merges and association changes are outside this enrichment plan. A routing decision that would move an account between teams requires separate approval even when the underlying evidence is accepted.

Use different states for malformed input, unresolved identity, provider failure and field ambiguity. A failed lookup must not erase a known website or profile. For a size range spanning two routing segments, retain the uncertainty and assign a reviewer rather than selecting the first matching branch. A future timeout following an update requires direct record inspection before a retry; rereading beforehand is a useful conflict check, not an atomic lock.

## Validation and operating measures

Test ordinary websites, country-code suffixes, meaningful subdomains, rebrands, shared parent domains, consumer-mailbox inputs and no-match cases. Include an employee range that spans a segment threshold. Confirm that identity acceptance and field acceptance are reported separately and that rejected candidates remain inspectable. Reconcile each input to a resolved or explicit exception state. The outcome to optimise is defensible account matches and usable routing evidence, not raw field fill rate. All validation described here is a future pilot specification, not demonstrated live HubSpot behaviour.
