Revenue data is decision-ready when a sales manager can tell who owns a deal, which buying process it represents and what evidence supports the next step. Filling every field is not the same thing. Begin with checks that change forecasting or handoffs, then distinguish a missing value from an unsupported commercial judgement.

## Deepline-specific context vs HubSpot adaptation

The Deepline source emphasises decision-critical fields, field ownership, dry-run proposals and aged exceptions. It references CRM integrations but does not establish the schema, sales stages or permissions of your HubSpot portal.

This adaptation anchors quality checks in HubSpot deals, companies, contacts and tasks. HubSpot documents these records and their APIs; the stage-specific checks, exception taxonomy and repair service-level targets below are recommended operating policies, not built-in guarantees of completeness or forecast accuracy.

## Prerequisites and licensing

Ask sales leadership to approve what each stage means and which fields are required for that stage. Obtain pipeline and stage internal IDs, property definitions, owner mappings and existing automation dependencies. The [deals API documentation](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/objects/deals/guide) explicitly distinguishes internal stage/pipeline IDs from display labels.

Start with an export or least-privilege read access. If native workflows are selected, verify a qualifying Professional or Enterprise subscription and supported workflow object/action under [Create workflows](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows). Data Hub Professional or Enterprise is required for custom code actions, according to [workflow actions](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/choose-your-workflow-actions). An external checker can avoid that specific dependency; it still needs secure hosting and explicitly authorised endpoint scopes.

## Build a deal-centred quality contract

Read `dealname`, `pipeline`, `dealstage`, `hubspot_owner_id`, `amount` and `closedate` alongside the business's approved next-step fields. Treat the company and contact associations as part of identity, not decoration. An apparently complete deal linked to the wrong legal entity is a serious failure.

Propose separate custom properties such as `revqa_state`, `revqa_primary_reason`, `revqa_first_detected_at`, `revqa_review_due_at` and `revqa_receipt_url`. They are not HubSpot defaults. Use [property definitions](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/properties/guide) to choose stable internal names, valid enumeration options and date types. Keep the full list of simultaneous defects in an external receipt so a single primary reason does not conceal secondary issues.

## Implementation steps

1. **Define rules by stage and pipeline.** An early discovery opportunity may legitimately have an unknown budget. A late procurement opportunity with no confirmed contracting entity needs review. Build a versioned rule matrix instead of one global missing-fields filter. Record the reason a field is required so operators can distinguish policy from preference.

2. **Validate identity and associations first.** Confirm the intended company and relevant buying contacts using record IDs. Read the actual [association definitions](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/associations/associate-records/guide). If several subsidiaries share a domain, ask the account owner to resolve the relationship instead of merging on a name or website match.

3. **Evaluate evidence freshness.** Compare the recorded next action with its supporting note, customer interaction date and internal review deadline. An old stage is a review signal, not proof the deal should be closed lost. Keep a missing expected customer date separate from an overdue internal task.

4. **Produce a repair proposal, not a forecast rewrite.** Show current value, rule, evidence, suggested action and accountable owner. Missing data should create a question for the rep. Do not set amount to zero, push `closedate` forward or demote `dealstage` to make a quality report look clean.

5. **Create an actionable exception queue.** A proposed task should explain the specific decision needed and link the affected deal. HubSpot's [tasks guide](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/legacy/crm/activities/tasks/guide) documents assignment through `hubspot_owner_id`, due time through `hs_timestamp` and associations. Reuse one open repair item for the same defect episode rather than creating a task every daily scan.

## Approval boundary

The data steward may approve deterministic normalisation of permitted custom metadata. The deal owner approves changes to commercial facts, and sales leadership owns policy changes that alter stage requirements. Changing ownership, amount, stage, lifecycle status or closing a deal is outside an unattended quality checker. Any eventual write must match a reviewed proposal revision and recheck for intervening seller edits.

## Failure handling and validation

Separate unsupported stage IDs, missing associations, inaccessible records, stale evidence and uncertain write outcomes. A permissions error is an unassessed record, not a clean one. Keep the first-detected timestamp through repeated scans; resetting it each day hides ageing. Close an exception only when its rule is satisfied or an authorised waiver has an expiry and rationale.

Offline fixtures should include an early-stage deal with legitimately unknown amount, two currencies, an obsolete stage ID, a changed owner, an inaccessible company and an overdue next step. Reconcile all scanned deals into assessed, excluded or failed categories. A later authorised pilot must inspect actual task associations and read back approved metadata. Report aged critical exceptions and reopened defects alongside coverage; neither a full field count nor a successful checker run proves reliable revenue data.
